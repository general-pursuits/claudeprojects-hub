# Opportunity Designed — LinkedIn / Social Aesthetic Guide & Competitive Research

*A read on how leading outdoor, run, and performance brands present themselves on LinkedIn, and what it means for Opportunity Designed's visual presence. Compiled July 2026 from peer company-page banners.*

---

## The headline finding

Every serious brand in this set treats the LinkedIn banner as a **single, full-bleed, edge-to-edge photograph with essentially no text on it.** The company name and tagline are left to LinkedIn's own text block directly below the banner. The banner's only job is **mood and emotion** — it earns attention, it doesn't explain.

This directly validates the "Option B / minimal" direction and pushes one step further: for a brand in this world, the strongest move is a **text-free photo**, not a headline graphic. The words already appear below; the image should do what words can't.

## What each peer does

**Satisfy** *(your stated inspiration)* — Full-bleed, high-contrast **black-and-white** photo of a trail runner mid-leap across granite. Editorial, moody, monochrome. Black square logo tile bottom-left. Zero banner text. The most "art-directed" of the set — it feels like a magazine spread, not an ad.

**Arc'teryx** — Full-bleed bright **alpine landscape**: ski-tourers ascending a snowy ridge under a big sky, tiny humans for scale. Natural, aspirational, real place. Black circular logo. No text. Sells the *environment*, not the product.

**Patagonia** — Full-bleed skier carving a massive snowy couloir. Natural cool blue-white grade, pure sense of place and scale. Iconic logo. No text.

**The North Face** — Full-bleed **dramatic mountaineering**: two roped climbers in blowing spindrift, warm figures against a cold storm. Epic, harsh, human-in-environment. White square logo. No text.

**norda** — Full-bleed **warm-toned motion blur** of runners in stride (earthy tan/red). The one exception: a small "norda" wordmark sits top-right *on* the image. White square logo bottom-left. Motion + warmth.

**Salomon** — Full-bleed **golden-hour** trail-runner silhouette on a ridge, with a duotone split (cool mountains, warm sunset, magenta cast). White logo. Energy + warmth. No text.

**New Balance** — Full-bleed **extreme product macro** — the leather and stitching of a grey "550," shot like texture. Desaturated cool grey. Red (brand-color) square logo. No text. Proof you don't even need a scene — just one thing, shot beautifully.

**Nike** — Full-bleed **dynamic motion montage** (multi-frame football/soccer, neon accents, crowd energy). Black square swoosh. The most mass-market and energetic — a useful *contrast*: it's louder and busier than the quiet, editorial tone the others (and your brand) favor.

## The patterns (what they share)

1. **Full-bleed photography, edge to edge.** No letterbox bars, no framed graphic. The image fills the banner.
2. **Little or no text.** Seven of eight run zero banner copy; norda has only a small wordmark. The name/tagline live in LinkedIn's text below, so the banner never repeats them.
3. **One strong subject** — a landscape, a human in motion in the environment, or a single product shot as texture. Never a collage of messages.
4. **A committed, consistent color grade** — B&W (Satisfy), warm golden (norda, Salomon), natural alpine (Arc'teryx, Patagonia, TNF), cool desaturated (New Balance). The grade *is* the brand signature.
5. **Logo bottom-left in a solid square** (white or brand color) overlapping the banner — so the lower-left of the image is treated as the logo's territory.
6. **Aspirational and emotional, not informational.** These banners make you *feel* the brand's world before you read a word.

## Aesthetic guide for Opportunity Designed

**Direction:** a **full-bleed, text-free, moody mountain photograph**, brand-toned. This fits the peer set *and* your own identity — desert/mountain, "see what others miss," the stippled-cloud mark, the Anton-and-purple system. You already own the imagery; the move is to let it run edge-to-edge and stay quiet.

**Color grade (your signature):** desaturated and **dark**, with your warm-charcoal cast rather than a cold one. Think Satisfy's editorial restraint, warmed toward `#2F2D2A`. Concretely: pull saturation ~50–70%, drop brightness so the image reads as a dark, textured field, hold enough contrast that the rock structure still reads. This matches the dark mountain image you selected — that mood is exactly right.

**Composition & the logo:** keep the **lower-left dark** (fade toward `#2F2D2A`) so your dark stipple-mark tile blends into the image instead of sitting on a mismatched patch — the seam the other brands avoid by using solid logo squares. Keep any focal detail (a peak, a ridge line) in the **center**, since LinkedIn crops toward the middle on mobile.

**Text:** none on the banner. LinkedIn already shows "Own your category. Lead the pace. Opportunity doesn't appear. It's built." directly beneath it — the banner should carry the *feeling*, the text block carries the *words*.

**Consistency across touchpoints:** the same dark-desert-mountain mood, Anton display type, and single purple accent should tie together the site hero, OG/share images, this banner, and any deck or one-pager — so every surface reads as one brand. Your live site already sets this tone; the social presence should match it, not diverge.

**Where you sit vs. the field:** Arc'teryx / Patagonia / TNF sell *the place*; Nike sells *energy*; New Balance sells *the object*. Your lane is closest to **Satisfy** — editorial, restrained, a little severe, human-scale. That's the right neighborhood for a strategist who "sees what others miss": quiet confidence, not loud performance.

## Banner spec (for production)

- **Size:** 4200 × 700 px (LinkedIn Company Page cover; 6:1). Keep file under 3 MB (PNG or JPEG).
- **Safe zone:** critical focal content in the **center** (survives mobile crop); nothing important in the **bottom-left** (logo) or the far top/bottom (can crop).
- **Background/fill color:** `#2F2D2A` (site charcoal) for any fade or blend — *not* `#1A1916`.
- **Logo:** dark stipple mark on a `#2F2D2A` tile; lower-left of the banner faded to the same charcoal so it blends.
