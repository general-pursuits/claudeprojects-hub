The headline finding

What they all do

Every serious brand in this set treats the banner as a single, full-bleed photograph with essentially no text on it. The name and tagline are left to LinkedIn’s own text block below. The banner’s only job is mood — it earns attention, it doesn’t explain. That validates going text-free, and letting one strong image do what words can’t.

The shared patterns

The rules underneath
  • Full-bleed photography, edge to edge. No letterbox bars, no framed graphic.
  • Little or no text. Seven of eight run zero banner copy; norda has only a small wordmark.
  • One strong subject — a landscape, a human in motion, or a single product shot as texture. Never a collage.
  • A committed colour grade — B&W, warm golden, natural alpine, cool desaturated. The grade is the signature.
  • Logo bottom-left in a solid square, overlapping the image. The lower-left is the logo’s territory.
  • Aspirational and emotional, not informational. You feel the brand before you read a word.

Your direction & colour grade

Where Opportunity Designed sits

Arc’teryx, Patagonia and TNF sell the place; Nike sells energy; New Balance sells the object. Your lane is closest to Satisfy — editorial, restrained, a little severe, human-scale. Quiet confidence, not loud performance.

The grade (the signature): a desaturated, dark, warm-charcoal black-and-white — cast toward #2F2D2A, never cold or bright-neutral. Keep highlights muted (no blown whites), shadows warm, and enough contrast that structure still reads. Composition: keep the focal moment (here, the fog dissolving at the ridgeline) centred — LinkedIn crops to the middle on mobile — and the lower-left dark so the stipple mark blends. Text: none — the tagline already shows below.

Layout & safe zones

How the banner has to behave

Production spec

For every banner going forward
Size: 4200 × 700 px (LinkedIn Company Page cover, 6:1). Under 3 MB — export JPEG for photos.
Safe zone: focal content centred; nothing important in the bottom-left (logo) or far top/bottom (crop).
Charcoal: #2F2D2A for any fade or fill — not #1A1916.
Grade: desaturated, dark, warm-charcoal B&W; muted highlights, warm shadows; subject still legible.
Text: none. LinkedIn shows the name + tagline below the banner.
