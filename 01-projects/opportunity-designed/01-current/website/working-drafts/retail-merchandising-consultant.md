# Retail Merchandising Consultant

A retail merchandising consultant helps a consumer brand decide what to sell, to whom, at what price, and how it shows up on the shelf, so a retailer says yes and shoppers keep reordering. I work from the buyer's side. I spent 10+ years as a retail buyer and merchandiser, so the plan reflects how a retailer actually reaches a yes-or-no decision.

## What a retail merchandising consultant does

Merchandising is the set of calls that decide whether your product earns space and holds it. The work covers four areas:

- Assortment and range: which SKUs go in, which come out, and how the line reads as a whole.
- Pricing and MAP: a price and margin structure a buyer can defend and that protects your brand at the shelf.
- Presentation: planograms, pack architecture, and the category story that makes a shopper reach for you.
- Sell-through and replenishment: what keeps reordering instead of getting marked down.

## How this differs from a broker or an in-house hire

A broker sells your line to buyers for a commission. That gets you in the room. A merchandising consultant builds the strategy behind the line, so the pitch holds up and the reorders follow. An in-house merchandiser makes sense once your volume is steady and predictable. Earlier than that, an outside buyer's read is faster and costs less than a full-time seat. If you are weighing the buying side specifically, see the retail buying consultant page.

## When to bring one in

A few signs it is time:

- You are getting buyer meetings but not the yes.
- Your margin slips every time you win an account.
- A category review or line review is coming and you get one shot.
- You are moving from direct-to-consumer into wholesale and the rules are new.

## What an engagement covers

Depending on where your brand is, the work can include a line review and assortment plan, a pricing and MAP framework, a retailer-ready pitch and line sheet, or a channel plan for getting from your current accounts to the ones you want. Every engagement starts from the buyer's decision and works backward.

## FAQ

**What does a retail merchandising consultant cost?** Pricing follows scope and outcomes rather than hours. Larger engagements are scoped to the work.

**What is the difference between merchandising and buying?** Buying is the retailer's decision to bring a product in. Merchandising is the wider set of calls, assortment, pricing, presentation, and sell-through, that decides whether it stays and grows. A consultant who has done the buying can align your merchandising to that decision.

**Do I need a broker or a consultant?** A broker opens doors for a commission. A consultant builds the strategy so the meeting converts and the reorders hold. Many brands use both, in that order.

**How do I sell to a retailer like Costco or Ulta?** It starts with a line a buyer can say yes to: the right assortment, a defensible price, and a plan for sell-through. The retailer guides on this site walk through specific accounts.
