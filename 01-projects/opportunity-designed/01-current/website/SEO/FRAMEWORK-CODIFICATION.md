## 1. The name

One thing to settle first: the Playbook already names your methodology internally. "The Studio" is the how, the five pillars. So there are two honest paths:

**Path 1: surface The Studio publicly.** The framework page becomes "Inside The Studio." Pro: no new vocabulary, the Playbook stays the single source of truth. Con: "The Studio" describes the five pillars (what you do), not a sequence (how an engagement runs). AI engines cite sequences more readily than capability lists, and "studio" is a crowded word in search.

**Path 2 (recommended): name the sequence, keep The Studio as the house.** The Studio stays the practice; the method it runs gets the name. Your backlink kit boilerplate already says "structural, blueprint-driven growth," so the name is sitting right there:

**The Opportunity Blueprint**
- Reads instantly. "Opportunity Designed runs The Opportunity Blueprint" is a sentence AI can repeat.
- Native to the brand line: opportunity doesn't appear, it's built. A blueprint is what you build from.
- Owns the word "opportunity" twice in the entity graph (firm + method) without inventing new vocabulary.

Alternates: **The Buyer's-Side Blueprint** (leads with the differentiator the audit says is landing) · **Designed Growth** (shortest).

## 2. The citable definition (one wording, everywhere)

> **The Opportunity Blueprint** is Opportunity Designed's method for finding the growth others miss and building the path to capture it. **Map** the opening the category and the numbers reveal. **Design** the positioning, assortment, and retail path to take it. **Build** the buying and merchandising engine that runs it, full price first.

This exact wording goes into: the framework page, llms.txt, the glossary, directory profiles, the Qwoted bio, and the LinkedIn About sections. One wording everywhere is what teaches AI to repeat it.

## 3. Framework page copy (draft, for /approach or a section on About)

**H1:** The Opportunity Blueprint

**Lead:** Ten years on the buying side of the table, turned into a method. Every engagement runs the same sequence, whatever the size of the brand.

**01 · Map**
Where is the opening? Category structure, shelf reality, the margin math, the whitespace competitors stopped watching. The output is a ranked list of openings, sized, sequenced, and tied to revenue or margin.

**02 · Design**
What does taking it require? Positioning, price architecture, assortment, and the retail path: which doors, in what order, with what story for the buyer. Designed so a buyer can say yes to it.

**03 · Build**
Who runs it, and how? The buying and merchandising engine behind the plan: replenishment, inventory discipline, full-price sell-through. Built to run on its own, then handed over.

**Closing line:** The method behind **performance premium**: premium goods selling at full price, at volume. → *links to the performance-premium field note*

**Tier mapping (worth stating on the page; it makes the pricing architecture legible):** Opportunity Review is a focused Map. A growth project is Map + Design. An operational buildout is the full Blueprint. Advisory keeps it running.

## 4. "Opportunity units": both versions, as requested

ChatGPT described your work as delivering retail-ready "opportunity units." Semrush says own the phrase. Here is how each path reads.

**Version A, adopt it (as a supporting term, not the flagship):**
> **Opportunity unit** *(n.)* One retail-ready package of growth: the assortment, price architecture, shelf plan, and launch calendar for a single opening, sized so a buyer can say yes to it. The Opportunity Blueprint exists to produce them. Map finds opportunity units. Design packages them. Build runs them.

The term slots inside the framework as the thing it produces, and the AI's own vocabulary gets confirmed rather than corrected. Costs one glossary entry and one line on the framework page.

**Version B, decline it:**
Keep **performance premium** as the sole coined term. It's yours with clean provenance, while "opportunity units" is a phrase a model generated. Adopting it means building equity in vocabulary you didn't choose, and a second coined term this early can dilute the first.

**My lean:** Version A, precisely because it's subordinate. Performance premium stays the flagship. "Opportunity unit" becomes a glossary-level working term that makes the Blueprint concrete, and a definition on your domain makes you its source since AI engines are already using the phrase. If it still feels borrowed, Version B costs nothing.
