## 1. Interested — Schedule a Call

Use when: The inquiry is in your wheelhouse and you want to talk.

**Subject:** Re: Your inquiry to Opportunity Designed

Hi [First Name],

Thanks for reaching out — [specific thing about their inquiry that caught your attention, e.g. "the category reset you described sounds like exactly the kind of challenge I dig into" or "I've worked on similar assortment builds for indie retailers"].

I'd like to learn more about what you're working on. Are you open to a 30-minute call this week or next? Here are a few times that work on my end:

- [Day, Time]
- [Day, Time]
- [Day, Time]

If none of those land, send me a couple that work for you and we'll make it happen.

Addie

## 2. Interested — But Need More Context First

Use when: You want to say yes but need more detail before committing a call.

**Subject:** Re: Your inquiry to Opportunity Designed

Hi [First Name],

Thanks for getting in touch. [Their area of interest] is something I spend a lot of time in, so you're in the right place.

Before we jump on a call, it would help me to understand a few things:

- What does the timeline look like? Is this tied to a season, a launch, or a broader planning cycle?
- Who's involved on your side? Is this a team effort or are you the primary decision-maker?
- Is there a budget range you're working within?

No need for a formal brief — a few sentences is plenty. That way I can come to our conversation with something useful instead of starting from scratch.

Addie

---

## 3. Good Fit — But Not Right Now

Use when: You're interested but at capacity or the timing doesn't work.

**Subject:** Re: Your inquiry to Opportunity Designed

Hi [First Name],

Thanks for reaching out — I appreciate the detail you shared about [their project/challenge].

I want to be upfront: my current engagement runs through [month/date], so I wouldn't be able to start meaningful work until [timeframe]. If that timeline works on your end, I'd be happy to set up a preliminary conversation now so we're ready to move when the window opens.

If you're on a tighter timeline, I understand. Either way, feel free to reach back out when the timing is right.

Addie

---

## 4. Not a Fit — Polite Pass

Use when: The inquiry is outside your expertise or not the kind of work you take on.

**Subject:** Re: Your inquiry to Opportunity Designed

Hi [First Name],

Thanks for reaching out. I read through your inquiry and want to be straightforward — [reason it's not a fit, e.g. "the scope you're describing sits more in the brand marketing space than the merchandising and buying strategy where I do my best work" or "I typically work with retailers and brands in the outdoor/specialty space, so I may not be the right fit for your category"].

That said, [optional: "if you're looking for someone in [their actual need], I'd suggest checking out [resource/person/direction]" or simply "I hope you find the right partner for this"].

Appreciate you thinking of Opportunity Designed.

Addie

## 5. Follow-Up After No Response

Use when: You sent a response 5–7 days ago and haven't heard back. One follow-up, then let it go.

**Subject:** Re: Your inquiry to Opportunity Designed

Hi [First Name],

Just circling back on this — wanted to make sure my last note didn't get buried. If you're still thinking through the [project/category work/engagement], I'm happy to pick up the conversation whenever the timing is right.

No pressure either way.

Addie

## 6. Post-Call — Sending a Proposal

Use when: You've had the discovery call and you're ready to send scope + pricing.

**Subject:** Opportunity Designed — Proposal for [Company Name]

Hi [First Name],

Good talking with you [today/yesterday/on Friday]. Attached is the proposal covering what we discussed — [one-line summary of scope, e.g. "a full assortment strategy engagement across your top three categories"].

A few things to flag:

- [Key detail or decision point, e.g. "I've outlined two tiers depending on how deep you want to go on competitive intelligence"]
- [Timeline note, e.g. "Timeline assumes a kick-off in the first week of [month]"]
- [Next step, e.g. "Take a look when you get a chance and let me know if anything needs adjusting"]

Happy to jump on a quick call if it's easier to walk through together.

Addie

## 7. Quick Acknowledgment

Use when: You got the inquiry but can't write a real response yet (busy day, traveling, etc). Buys you time without leaving them hanging.

**Subject:** Re: Your inquiry to Opportunity Designed

Hi [First Name],

Got your note — thanks for the detail. I want to give this a proper read and get back to you with something thoughtful. Expect to hear from me by [day, e.g. "end of day Thursday"].

Addie

---

## Tips for Using These

- **Always personalize the first line.** Reference something specific they said. Generic openers read like auto-replies.
- **Keep it short on mobile.** These are already trimmed for phone screens — resist the urge to add more.
- **Sign off as "Addie" not "Best, Addie Morrow."** You're a person, not a department. Full name + title is in your email signature already.
- **One follow-up max.** If they don't respond to the follow-up, they're not interested. Move on.
- **Update the tracker.** After sending any of these, update the Status column in your Google Sheet (Contacted, In Conversation, Proposal Sent, etc.).
