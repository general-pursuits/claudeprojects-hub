# Field Note Voice — v1 draft for your ear

This sits on top of the locked Opportunity Designed voice. It does not replace it. It adds the personality layer you described: smart, relatable, chill, respected, humble but does not hedge, navigates corporate in an alt style, never a robot.

## Who you are writing to

The person who can say yes. Founders, VPs of sales, heads of merchandising at consumer brands roughly $2M to $50M, and the buyers and category managers on the retail side. They are sharp, short on time, and allergic to being sold. They can smell a content-marketing blog from the headline.

So the voice is not "marketer explaining retail." It is the buyer who left the chair and will tell you how the room actually works, because there is nothing to sell you that the truth does not already close.

## The voice in one line

Talks like the smartest person at the table who is not trying to be. Plain, unhurried, a little edge. Respects your intelligence enough to skip the pitch.

## The six moves that make it

1. **Talk from the chair.** First person when it earns its place. "I sat in the line review" beats "buyers conduct line reviews." You were the buyer. That is the whole advantage, so use the "I."
2. **Short sentence after a long one.** Let it land. The unhurried McConaughey rhythm is mostly this: a long observation, then four words that stick.
3. **One plain-spoken edge per section.** Say the thing other people soften. Not crude, just unhedged. The reader trusts you more for it.
4. **Concrete over clever.** A pallet, a freight number, a Tuesday reset. Never "leverage synergies." Specifics are what make it sound like a person who did the job.
5. **Warmth without softness.** No hype, no flattery, no fear-selling. You are on their side and you are not worried about it.
6. **Personality in the connective tissue, discipline in the answers.** This is the trick that lets it stay cool and still win search. The short-answer block and the FAQ answers stay clean and quotable so AI engines lift them cleanly. The swagger lives in the openers, the transitions, and the insight lines.

## What does not bend, even for voice

- **No em dashes.** Colon, comma, or period.
- **Answer first** in every short-answer block and FAQ. LLM lift and featured snippets depend on it.
- **No client numbers.** Proof comes from public facts: firsts, durations, placements, counts.
- **No fixed-deliverable promises, no AI evangelism, no consultant abstraction**, and the banned words stay banned (gut check, honest, free, absolute "never").
- **Cool is fine, sloppy is not.** One note on "doesn't fuck around": in here, that is exactly the register. In published copy aimed at corporate buyers, I would carry that energy through stance and rhythm, not profanity, because a VP of merchandising reads a curse word on a consultant's site as a credibility leak, not as cool. My recommendation is to keep the published page clean and let the edge come from what you are willing to say, not the words you swear with. You can overrule this and I will dial it up.

## Where the personality can and cannot go

| Block | Voice setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Short answer / TLDR | Clean, warm, tight | This is what AI engines quote. Keep it liftable. |
| Section openers | Full voice | Where you hook a skimming exec. |
| Body + insight lines | Full voice, one edge each | Where you prove you sat in the chair. |
| FAQ answers | Clean, answer-first | Schema and snippet capture. |
| CTA | Plain, no pressure | Pressure reads as need. |

## Three before / after samples

These are written fresh so they touch nothing locked. The "before" is your current buttoned voice. The "after" is the target.

### 1. Short answer block (stays mostly clean by design)

**Before:** Getting into Costco starts with a regional buyer, not a national one. You pitch one region, prove the item moves at club volume and club price, and let that sell-through earn the expansion. Costco buys few items deep, so the question is rarely whether your line is good.

**After:** There is no getting into Costco. There is getting into one region, in front of one buyer, with one item that earns the rest. You prove it moves at club volume and club price, then let the sell-through make your next argument for you. Costco goes deep on few things. Give them the one thing.

### 2. Section opener (full voice)

**Before:** Costco's buying process is built around a regional structure. Each region has its own buyer with the authority to bring in local items before a national rollout.

**After:** Here is the part most brands get backwards. The national rollout everyone daydreams about is not the door. It is the thing you back into, one strong region at a time. I sat across that table. The buyer in front of you is not deciding whether America wants your product. They are deciding whether their region does, this quarter, at that price.

### 3. Insight paragraph (full voice, and a live scrub)

**Before:** It is important to ensure your packaging is optimized for club-store logistics before pitching, as this can significantly impact buyer decisions.

**After:** Before you pitch, look at your pallet, not your deck. A club buyer is doing freight math in their head while you talk. If your case pack fights the pallet, or your item needs its own reset, that is a cost they see and you do not. Fix the box before you book the meeting. The story only gets a hearing if the math already works.

*(The "after" also drops two tells the scrub would catch: "it is important to ensure" and "optimized." Cleaner and more like you at the same time.)*

## The dial

Same voice, three intensities. Pick one for field notes. LinkedIn can run a notch hotter than the site.

- **Buttoned** — moves 1, 4, 5 only. Reads sharp and clean, low swagger. Safest with conservative buyers.
- **Balanced** — all six moves, one edge per section, first person where it earns it. My recommendation for the site. The samples above are set here.
- **Loose** — more first person, more rhythm, more edge. Great on LinkedIn, a little much for a stakeholder landing on your MAP pricing page cold.
