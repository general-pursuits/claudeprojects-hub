# Safety, Privacy, and Property Access

These rules are foundational. Insurance and legal review may require stronger rules.

## Dog safety

- Require disclosure of bite history, escape behavior, reactivity, medical concerns, and handling restrictions.
- Decline or pause service when the equipment, environment, dog behavior, weather, or walker condition is unsafe.
- Carry waste bags, water when conditions require it, a charged phone, and emergency contact details.
- Establish heat, cold, smoke, ice, and severe-weather thresholds before launch.
- Never punish, strike, alpha-roll, choke, or intentionally frighten a dog.
- Use only handling methods and equipment explicitly accepted within the service policy.

## Home and building access

- Store access instructions in the minimum necessary secure system.
- Never label a physical key with a full address or unit number.
- Never share door codes, lockbox details, resident directories, parking access, or client schedules.
- Confirm that doors, gates, crates, and alarms are returned to the documented state before leaving.
- Do not enter rooms or handle property outside the agreed service area except in an emergency.
- Report lost keys or suspected access exposure immediately.

## Client and location privacy

- Never post unit numbers, access points, keys, mail, visible documents, license plates, or identifying interiors.
- Do not post in real time when doing so could reveal that a client is away.
- Obtain separate permission for private report photos and public social use.
- A client declining social media use must receive the same service quality.
- Remove location metadata when appropriate and avoid recognizable security details in images.

## Walker privacy

- Use a separate business phone number, email, booking identity, and public profile.
- Do not publish a home address.
- If presenting a background check, prefer a reputable verification badge or controlled report-sharing method. Never distribute a casually redacted report containing reversible or overlooked personal information.
- Keep personal calendar details private; the public booking page should expose only available service times.

## Incidents

Create a written incident record for any bite, injury, escape, missing dog, property damage, access exposure, confrontation, or emergency veterinary visit. Record facts, times, actions, witnesses, communications, and available evidence without speculation.

## Before accepting paid clients

Validate and document:

- Appropriate pet-care business insurance and bonding options
- Business registration and tax obligations
- Service agreement and liability terms
- Emergency veterinary authorization
- Background-check presentation method
- Building rules regarding commercial access, solicitation, and shared pet areas
- Secure client-data and key-management process
